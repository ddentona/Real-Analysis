\section{Problem 5.3}
    For each of the following, provide a proof or a  counterexample. 
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item   If $A$ and $B$ are compact, must $A \cup B$ be compact?
        \item   If $A$ and $B$ are compact, must $A \cap B$ be compact?
    \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{Solution (a)}
        \begin{proof}
            By the Heine-Borel Theorem, if $A$ and $B$ are compact, the two of them are closed and bounded.
            We can name bounds for the two of these and recall the definition of being closed (having an open complement).
            \begin{gather}
                \forall a \in A, L_A \leq a \leq U_A\\
                \forall b \in B, L_B \leq b \leq U_B\\
                \forall x \in A^c, \exists \delta > 0 : (x - \delta, x + \delta) \subseteq A^c\\
                \forall x \in B^c, \exists \delta > 0 : (x - \delta, x + \delta) \subseteq B^c
            \end{gather}

            Suppose we define the union of $A$ and $B$ and its complement by DeMorgan's Law.
            \begin{gather}
                A \cup B = \left\{ c \vert c \in A \vee c \in B \right\}\\
                (A \cup B)^c = A^c \cap B^c
            \end{gather}

            Since both $A^c$ and $B^c$ are open and the intersection of a finite number of open sets is also open, $A^c \cap B^c$ is open.
            By extension, $(A \cup B)^c$ is open and $A \cup B$ is closed.

            Suppose that $U = \max(U_A, U_B)$ and $L = \min(L_A, L_B)$.
            \begin{gather}
                \forall a \in A, U \geq U_A \geq a\\
                \forall b \in B, U \geq U_B \geq b\\
                \forall a \in A, L \leq L_A \leq a\\
                \forall b \in B, L \leq L_B \leq b
            \end{gather}

            From this, we conclude that $U$ is an upper bound of $A \cup B$ and $L$ is a lower bound of $A \cup B$.
            This means that $A \cup B$ is bounded.
            Since $A \cup B$ is both closed and bounded, then by the Heine-Borel Theorem, it is compact.
        \end{proof}

    \subsection{Solution (b)}
        \begin{proof}
            By the Heine-Borel Theorem, if $A$ and $B$ are compact, the two of them are closed and bounded.
            This means that $A^c$ and $B^c$ are open.
            This means that $A^c \cup B^c$, which can be turned into $(A \cap B)^c$ by DeMorgan's Laws, is open.
            $A \cap B$ is closed.

            We can write the definition of $A \cap B$ and name bounds for the two of these.
            \begin{gather}
                A \cap B = \left\{ c \vert c \in A \andtxt c \in B \right\}\\
                \forall a \in A, L_A \leq a \leq U_A\\
                \forall b \in B, L_B \leq b \leq U_B\\
                \forall c \in A \cap B, L_A \leq c \leq U_A \andtxt L_B \leq c \leq U_B
            \end{gather}

            Since there is a minimum and maximum bound of $c$, we can say that $A \cap B$ is bounded.
